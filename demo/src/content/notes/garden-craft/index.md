A digital garden stays alive on one condition: that touching it is cheap.
The moment "fix that sentence" means *clone, branch, edit, commit, push,
wait for CI*, the sentence stays broken. This site's answer is to make the
reading surface and the writing surface the same surface — see [[editing]].

## Small notes, linked densely

A garden note earns its keep by being small enough to finish and linked
well enough to be found. Prefer five linked notes to one sprawling page;
let [[wikilinks]] carry the structure instead of a table of contents. Dead
links are part of the method — each one is a note you owe yourself.

## Pruning

Old notes rot in two ways: they become wrong, or they become orphans.
Wrongness is the easy case — edit the offending block where you see it, and
the journal remembers what it used to say ([[revisions]] has the details).
Orphans need pruning: link them in, merge them down, or delete them without
guilt. A garden is judged by what it keeps alive, not by what it has
accumulated.

## Let the tools carry the ceremony

Commits, history, backups — the ceremony is real, but it shouldn't be
yours. With `autocommit` on, saves become git commits on their own;
imports arrive from the [[inbox]] on their own; even translations are one
click in [[ai-assist]].
What's left for you is the only part that was ever interesting: having
something to say.
